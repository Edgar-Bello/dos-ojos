# SYNTHETIC THERMAL — NO CAMERA TOOK THESE PICTURES

Everything in this folder was **generated by a script**, `make_thermal.py`. No
drone flew, no thermal camera existed, and nothing here was measured. It is not
public data and it is not our data: it is a test pattern.

It exists because `dosojos-drone thermal` is an optional step most growers will
never use, and we could not otherwise show it running. We have no thermal camera
and no drone licence. Every figure it produces carries a **SYNTHETIC TEST DATA,
NOT A REAL FIELD** band, because the flight is registered with
`--source synthetic`, and nothing from this folder may be shown without it.

## What the script builds

Over a field outline you give it (here `PUBLIC-rgv-corn-1`, a real Valley corn
field from the USDA crop map — only its **shape** is borrowed):

| raster | what it holds |
|---|---|
| `data/chm.tif` | canopy height: ridges on 30-inch rows, 2 m tall, bare furrow between. `--pattern orchard` makes round crowns on a 6 m grid instead. |
| `data/thermal.tif` | canopy temperature in Celsius: leaves near 31 °C, sunlit soil 18 °C hotter as it really is, noise smoothed over about a metre, and **one round patch 30 m across running 3.5 °C warm**. |

The warm patch is the planted answer. A run that does not find it, or that finds
several, is a bug in the thermal step, not in the data.

## What the thermal step makes of it

```
canopy measured     29.7 acres
canopy temperature  31.0 C, spread 0.3 C
running hot         1.7% of the canopy

1. 85% chance it is a pest or disease - north-east corner, 2,795 m2, +3.5 C
   - runs 3.5 C above the rest of the canopy
   - covers 2795 m2
   - is a blob rather than a streak along the rows
   - is hot while the field still has water
   Go and look at it: the camera cannot name what it is.
```

One patch, in the right corner, at the right temperature, and **no false
alarms** — the smoothed noise clumps into warm blobs of 30 to 60 m², and the
size floor (the larger of 25 m² and 0.2% of the canopy) throws every one of
them out.

## What the percentage is, and is not

It is a **scorecard we wrote**, not a calibrated probability and not a
diagnosis. There is no labelled set of infested Valley fields to fit a real
model to, so the number exists to rank patches and to show the reasoning. Every
patch carries the signs behind its score so a grower can disagree with it. The
score is floored at 10% and capped at 85%: it is never allowed to sound certain.

A hot patch has three common causes and thermal alone cannot separate them —
the crop is thirsty, the water never reached that spot, or something is eating
it. So the score leans on the rest of Dos Ojos: the ground model from `terrain`
(a high spot argues *against* a pest), the water checkbook from the satellite
half (a field that is due water anyway argues against one), and the colour
camera's own flags (damage it can also see argues *for* one).

## Running it

```
run_demo.cmd
```

from PowerShell, or `bash run_demo.sh`. It writes `data/`, then `workspace/`
with `out/DEMO-corn-thermal/thermal.png`, `thermal.json` and
`thermal_patches.geojson`.

Replace this folder the day we can borrow a real radiometric camera, or find a
public thermal set. Nothing in the code depends on it.

## Looking for a real one, 2026-09-16

Searched for a free public dataset of a crop under a thermal camera. **Nothing
found that we can actually use**, and the reason is worth writing down, because it
is the same reason every time.

The step needs two things over the same field: **radiometric thermal**, meaning
every pixel carries a temperature and not a colour, and **something to build a canopy
height model from**, because the first thing `thermal` does is throw away
everything that is not a leaf. Sunlit soil runs about 20 °C above the crop, so
without a canopy mask the furrows are every warm patch on the field. Datasets
routinely have one and not the other.

| Candidate | Why not |
|---|---|
| [Vineyard RGB + multispectral + thermal](https://zenodo.org/records/18678620), Zenodo, CC BY, 17.8 GB | The thermal is **JPEG pictures, not temperatures**. `to_celsius` would refuse it, correctly. |
| [TERRA-REF](https://terraref.org/data/access-data.html) - Maricopa, **sorghum**, FLIR SC615, public domain, and a laser scanner for canopy height | The right data, and the right crop. The imagery is only on **Globus and needs an account**, which we cannot open on anyone's behalf. Its [Dryad deposit](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.4b8gtht99) (800 MB) is catalogues and traits, no imagery. |
| FLAME 3 | Radiometric TIFFs paired with RGB, which is exactly the shape we want - but it is **wildfire, not crops**. |
| [Zenmuse XT aerial set](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/ffgxxzx298/1), Mendeley, CC BY | A university campus, not a field. |

**The one to chase is TERRA-REF.** A free Globus account, the `terra-public`
endpoint, and `ua-mac/Level_1/ir_geotiff` for a sorghum season gives real canopy
temperature over the same sorghum the Purdue demo already covers, with a real
canopy height model beside it. That is an account for the account's owner to make,
not for us.

Until then this folder stays, and stays labelled.
